\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit our manuscript ``When Do Simple Models Win? Machine Learning Architectures for UV Absorption Prediction and Insights for General Molecular Property Prediction'' for consideration as an Article in \textit{Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling}.

We benchmark five ML models spanning Random Forest, XGBoost, Graph Neural Network (GNN), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and Transformer based deep learning models for UV $\lambda_{\max}$ prediction using identical data splits and evaluation protocols. Our main findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Simple vs.\ deep models fill different roles.} RF and the GNN are statistically tied on cross-validation (RMSE 31.34 vs.\ 31.69~nm, $p = 0.77$), but experimental validation on 16 novel compounds reverses the ranking: deep learning models outperform RF on out-of-distribution molecules (Transformer MAE = 26.3 vs.\ RF 38.5~nm).

    \item \textbf{Solvent encoding helps all models.} Concatenating solvent SMILES or fingerprints gives 6--8\% RMSE reductions across all architectures ($p < 0.02$), with no domain-specific descriptors needed.

    \item \textbf{Consistent interpretability.} RF feature importance and RNN gradient saliency both highlight the same chromophore motifs, confirming that both architectures pick up on real photochemistry.

    \item \textbf{Local inductive bias matters.} Models with a built-in locality constraint (fingerprints, message passing, RNNs) outperform the global-attention Transformer on this locally determined property.
\end{enumerate}

This work fits JCIM because controlled multi-architecture comparisons with out-of-distribution experimental validation are still rare in molecular ML. The practical takeaway is straightforward: use fingerprint models for screening within known chemical space, deep learning for novel scaffolds, and encode solvent identity for improved predictions across all model families.

All code, trained models, and datasets will be released publicly upon publication.

A preprint was deposited on ChemRxiv on March 25, 2026 (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15001242/v1}{10.26434/chemrxiv.15001242/v1}). The submitted version is the same as the preprint; formatting has been adapted for JCIM.

The manuscript has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript.
