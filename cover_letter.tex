\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ``When Do Simple Models Win? Machine Learning Architectures for UV Absorption Prediction'' for consideration as an Article in the \textit{Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling}.

This work provides a systematic, controlled benchmark of five machine learning approaches---Random Forest, XGBoost, Chemprop (D-MPNN), BiGRU, and ChemBERTa---spanning fingerprint, graph, recurrent, and Transformer architectures for predicting UV absorption wavelengths ($\lambda_{\max}$), using identical data splits, preprocessing, and evaluation protocols across all models.
Our key findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Complementary strengths of simple and deep models.} Random Forest with Morgan fingerprints and the Chemprop graph neural network achieve statistically indistinguishable cross-validated performance (RMSE = 31.34 and 31.69~nm, $p = 0.77$), both outperforming sequence-based models. However, experimental validation on 16 novel UV-absorbing compounds reverses this ranking: both deep learning models outperform RF on out-of-distribution molecules (ChemBERTa MAE = 26.3 vs.\ RF 38.5~nm).

    \item \textbf{Practical solvent encoding.} A simple SMILES/fingerprint concatenation strategy for encoding solute--solvent pairs yields consistent 6--8\% RMSE reductions across all model families, without requiring domain-specific descriptor engineering.

    \item \textbf{Interpretability across architectures.} We demonstrate that RF feature importance and BiGRU gradient saliency independently highlight the same chromophore motifs, confirming that both architectures learn meaningful photochemistry through different mechanisms.

    \item \textbf{Locality-of-property hypothesis.} We propose that models encoding local structural information (fingerprints, graph message passing, RNNs) outperform global-attention architectures on properties determined by local molecular features, providing a framework for model selection that generalizes beyond UV absorption.
\end{enumerate}

We believe this work is well suited for JCIM because it addresses a critical gap in molecular ML: the lack of controlled, multi-architecture comparisons with both in-distribution benchmarks and out-of-distribution experimental validation.
The practical guidance for model selection---choose fingerprint models for interpolation within known chemical space, deep learning for extrapolation to novel scaffolds---is directly relevant to JCIM readers working on molecular property prediction, virtual screening, and pharmaceutical development.

All code, trained models, and datasets will be made publicly available upon publication.
The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere.
All authors have approved the manuscript.

\closing{Sincerely,}
